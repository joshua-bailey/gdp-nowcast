Frequently Asked Questions

What does this dashboard show?

The dashboard has one headline nowcast of annualised quarterly US real GDP growth: Nowcast 2.0 · Direct, the Bayesian dynamic factor model. Components is a separate expenditure-side structural cross-check and Composite is the fixed ⅔ Direct + ⅓ Components point blend. They help assess the headline rather than competing with it for headline status. Atlanta Fed GDPNow and the NY Fed Staff Nowcast are external benchmarks, not ensemble members.

Why is Direct the headline model?

Direct is the selected W2.3 Bayesian DFM. It performed strongly and stably in the common-origin evaluation while retaining a coherent predictive density and an exact release-level news decomposition. Those features make it the operational anchor even when another point forecast is temporarily more accurate.

How does the Direct news decomposition reconcile?

For each week, the stacked bars add to the diamond and to the unrounded change in the Direct nowcast. Releases are processed in publication-date order; observations published on the same day enter jointly in the calculation but retain separate lines in the table. Revisions appear in one expandable Data revisions row. Model update shows the separate effect of refreshing the model's treatment of unusual observations and, when applicable, re-estimating it. That nonlinear effect cannot be uniquely divided among data released together, so it is not assigned to an observation. Publication stops if the observation formulas, release-event path, weekly total or displayed categories fail to reconcile within numerical tolerance.

What does Model update mean?

It combines two effects that are not data news. First, Bayesian outlier updating reassesses whether unusual readings are common economic signal or temporary noise specific to one series. Second, a full re-estimation, when run, can revise coefficients, factor dynamics and other model parameters. These effects are kept separate from Data revisions. They are not assigned to individual observations because the nonlinear update cannot be uniquely split among observations released together.

What is the role of the Components model?

Components is a near-peer structural cross-check. It separately models consumption, fixed investment, inventories, government, exports and imports, with explicit accounting identities. It has generally added the most relative value during volatile trade and inventory episodes, particularly post-COVID, but it has not dominated in every period. Its chart shows component evolution; it does not claim a release-level news decomposition. The selected specification uses a fixed-gold treatment, so historical tests that use today's gold history are labelled pseudo real time rather than true vintage.

Why does the Composite use ⅔ Direct and ⅓ Components?

Direct receives the majority weight because it is the primary coherent model. Components has different forecast errors and improves robustness in volatile periods. The split was fixed before the final combined evaluation and materially improved Direct across the evaluated samples. A stronger post-COVID 50/50 sensitivity is disclosed but was not selected ex post. Composite is point-only: it has no synthetic density, news decomposition or component attribution.

What do the uncertainty bands show?

Direct and Components report model-generated 50%, 68% and 90% posterior-predictive intervals. Their ordering, finiteness and economic plausibility are checked before publication. Composite has no interval because combining marginal forecast distributions without their joint dependence would create a false precision claim.

How is the backtest computed?

The historical tables compare forecasts at common 8-week, 4-week and final origins against both the BEA advance and third estimates. Rankings use only quarters available for every model in the selected view. The broad archive is 2013Q1–2019Q4, the fully live official comparison begins 2016Q3, and the post-COVID sample begins 2021Q1. The 2020 disruption is retained internally but excluded from formal rankings. Gold-dependent component results are labelled pseudo real-time.

Where did the retired models go?

Original DFM, Bayesian DFM v1, LASSO bridge, Chronos-2, the legacy equal-weight ensemble and BISTRO no longer produce current forecasts or receive new live scores. Their frozen historical results remain in the Backtest so the production decision stays auditable.

Why can dashboard GDP growth differ slightly from BEA's published number?

The modelling system uses 400× log-difference SAAR, the standard convention in much of the nowcasting literature, while BEA publishes compound annualised rates. The difference is normally small and every model is scored against a consistently constructed target.
