Backtest 2 · Multi-model true-vintage backtest (beta models)

A second, different backtest: instead of validating the production DFM alone, this one pits four independent models and their equal-weight ensemble against each other on identical real-time data, to test whether combining models beats any single one. A standalone research piece over two eras: post-GFC (2010Q4–2019Q4, a fixed pre-COVID window) and post-COVID (2021Q1 onward, the full record, which extends as each new quarter graduates). The post-COVID board also scores the Atlanta Fed GDPNow as an external real-time benchmark (it has no usable real-time vintage history for the pre-COVID window, so it appears post-COVID only). The rolling 8-quarter zoom-in is the Live Horserace.

Lowest-RMSE model: Ensemble post-GFC, Atlanta Fed GDPNow post-COVID. The ensemble's edge over the production DFM is directional post-GFC (Diebold-Mariano p=0.14) and directional post-COVID (p=0.12, n=22). No difference from the production DFM is statistically significant at this sample size, and short-window comparisons have low power against temporary performance shifts (Iacone, Rossini & Viselli 2024). The post-COVID board also scores the Atlanta Fed GDPNow as an external benchmark, on the same origins as our models from its real-time ALFRED vintages; because GDPNow freezes at each advance release, its value is its genuine pre-release nowcast (leakage-free). It has no usable real-time history before the post-COVID window, so it is not on the post-GFC board.

Two toggles. Horizon shows the board 8 weeks out, 4 weeks out, or the day before the release; each horizon is scored on common support (every model on the same quarters), so the post-COVID sample shrinks to 20 (8wk) / 21 (4wk) quarters. That truncation is a GDPNow limitation: it cannot start nowcasting a quarter until the prior quarter’s advance GDP prints, and the 2025–26 government-shutdown delays to the BEA calendar left it with no early-horizon estimate for the most recent quarters — whereas our models nowcast continuously and do cover them (a genuine timeliness disadvantage of GDPNow). Score vs re-scores the same nowcasts against the final (third) GDP estimate instead of the advance print, which separates genuine nowcast error from advance-print noise (the advance→third revision is itself ~0.76pp RMS over the 20 scored post-COVID quarters; quarters without a third estimate yet are dropped from that view, through 2026Q1). Post-GFC is a fixed pre-COVID window; post-COVID extends as the Live Horserace scores each graduated quarter.

GDP growth rates on this page are 400×log-difference SAAR; BEA’s published compound SAAR differs by <0.05pp in normal quarters (up to ~0.2pp in extreme ones). Scoring uses the same convention for every model.

Low/negative error correlations (above) are why combining helps: the members make largely independent errors, so averaging cancels much of each one's noise.

Live Horserace · our models vs the official nowcasts

A live, rolling scoreboard over the most recent 8 graduated quarters of the production DFM, the beta models (Tuned Bayesian DFM, LASSO bridge, Chronos-2), the alpha model BISTRO (even less tested than the betas — its record starts at the race's 2024Q1 floor with no backtest; see the FAQ) and the equal-weight ensemble of the DFM and the three betas, against the two headline official US nowcasts — the Atlanta Fed GDPNow and the NY Fed Staff Nowcast. All scored true point-in-time, on identical origins, vs the advance GDP print. Use the toggle to see each horizon through the quarter: our models lead at the earlier horizons (8 and 4 weeks out) (within statistical noise); the officials pull ahead only at the day-before origin.
