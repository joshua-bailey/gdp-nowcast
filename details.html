The equal-weight ensemble is the most accurate in both regimes. The ensemble's edge over the DFM is statistically significant post-GFC (Diebold-Mariano p=0.10) and directional post-COVID (p=0.18, a short 16-quarter sample).

The ensemble wins because its members make largely independent errors (low/negative correlations above), so averaging cancels much of each one's noise.

CRPS generalises MAE to the whole predictive distribution (lower is better); coverage compares the realised hit-rate to the band’s nominal level. DFM, Tuned Bayesian DFM and the ensemble join once their bands are logged.

Scored on the day-before-release ("final") horizon against the advance GDP estimate. Diebold-Mariano p is the test that a model's accuracy differs from the DFM's; small samples make it conservative early on. Each model holds out the target quarter's GDP, so the comparison is leakage-free. Note: Chronos-2 (8/9) scored on fewer quarters (a model run failed for a quarter), so rankings mix slightly different samples — read ties cautiously.
